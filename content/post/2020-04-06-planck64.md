My first hand-wired 40% ortholinear keyboard inspired by the Commodore C64. The Commodore C128-D was the first computer I had in my own room as a child. But the C64 keycaps and case shape are much more iconic, so I went with it.

## Highlights

In 2019 I went deeper into the rabbit hole of custom keyboards and found [Matt3os Blog](https://matt3o.com/book/) and countless pictures of nice, real custom boards. Intrigued by this, I designed two layouts using the [keyboard layout editor](http://www.keyboard-layout-editor.com/) and exported the initial CAD file using the [plate and case builder](http://builder.swillkb.com/). I modified the plate with LibreCad and added the screw holes and adjusted the corner radius to match the diameter of my router bits. Finding a company that offered laser cutting of brass for a reasonable price was not easy, but in the end I found one and ordered the plates.

Details of the building process are in the image section below.

## Layouts

The layout is basically the same as the one I use on my [OLKB Planck](https://keebs.fehmer.info/post/2020-04-05-planck/#layouts). The only difference is the four extra function keys on the right which are mapped to F8, F5, F6 and F7 which I use in my IDE for debugging.

## Firmware

## Images

### The case

I started with a slab of walnut, already straightened and in the final thickness of 22mm. Using a hand-router, I made a cutout of ~10mm matching exactly the brass plate and a deeper ~8mm cutout with a 2mm inset of the plate shape, giving it a nice ridge to sit on. Leaving only around 4mm of wood at the bottom was quite scary, but it turned out fine. Unfortunately, due to the height of the inner cutout, I was not able to add a USB connector, so I had to solder the cable directly. I added an aviator 5-pin connector to have it somewhat detachable. 

{{<image src="images/p64/2.jpg.webp" >}}

On the backside there is an additional cutout under the wrist rest for the brass weight of around 750 grams. I glued in seven brass screw sockets and used PCB spacers to attach the plate to the case. 

I finished the case by rounding off the edges, sanding the wood up to 320 grit and oiling it.

### Wiring

The Planck 64 is basically a Planck with 4 extra keys. With the ortholinear layout, the wiring was quite simple.

{{<image src="images/p64/wiring.svg" >}}

Inspired by other builders, I wanted to make the wiring as clean and beautiful as I could. To match the "vintage" look of wood and brass, I used thick, lacquer-insulated copper wire for the rows and a thinner, green silk-insulated wire for the columns. For the connections to the controller, I used an old floppy cable, which is the most modern wire used but essential to avoid a mess of cables.

{{<image src="images/p64/3.jpg.webp" >}}

For the first hand-wired keyboard and given I am not a huge electronic guy I am quite pleased with the result. I added a magnetic reed switch as a reset button to allow firmware flashing without deconstructing the whole keyboard.

{{<image src="images/p64/4.jpg.webp" >}}

### Assembly

With the case and "board" completed, it's time for the assembly. The inset is very precise and the plate will not fall out, even if not secured by screws.
